## Hero pattern
Plate sits BESIDE the headline: a split hero, roughly 40/60. The left 40% is a pale grey panel holding, top-aligned and left-aligned, the headline in a heavy black serif (Dwell's display face, about 48px, four to five lines), the dek in a plain grey sans about 17px, a "Text by" label in small grey sans with the author name in bold below, and then a boxed thumbnail strip (three small crops) with a full-width blue "View 15 Photos" button. The right 60% is a landscape photo (about 3:2, cropped to the panel height of roughly 680px at 1900) with a small square icon button in its bottom-right corner. No kicker on the article (the index pages carry a tiny "Articles" label). No caption on the hero; the credit appears only in the lightbox. No date at the top; "Published August 12, 2026" appears at the very bottom with the author bio. Headline never sits over the image, but it does sit on a tinted panel.

## Body column
Single centred column about 950px wide at 1900 (689px in the screenshot), left-aligned ragged right, large serif body at about 22px with loose leading, giving roughly 70-75 characters per line. No drop cap. The body opens with a short black dash rule (about 45px wide, 2px thick) as the opener device, then on the feature an italic series note ("Houses We Love: Every day we feature..."), then a bold serif H3 "Project Details:" followed by bold-label lines (Location, Architect, Footprint, Builder, Structural Engineer, Photographer), then "From the Architect:" and the entire body is an italic quoted statement. The guide's first paragraph is set like the rest, no lede treatment. Links are underlined. Grey full-width advertisement bands interrupt every three or four paragraphs.

## Image rhythm
Feature ("Houses We Love"): all text first (spec list + architect statement), then all fifteen plates stacked in sequence at the end, each a wide contained landscape or portrait (about 1420px wide at 1900, wider than the 950px text column on both sides) with a credit line "Photo by Peter Molloy" under each, no descriptive caption. Guide: text-led; a plate every three to five paragraphs. Widths alternate: column-width portrait (about 950px) and wide contained landscape (about 1420px). Captions on the guide are descriptive sentences in a medium-weight sans about 15px, left-aligned to the text column edge (not the plate edge), with "Image by Bob Stefko" in small grey sans on a second line. Every plate carries two circular icon buttons (expand bottom-left, heart bottom-right). No side-by-side pairs, no image beside text. A boxed grey "Get the Dwell Newsletter" module with a blue button appears once mid-article, and a product card (title, price, description, black pill "Shop" button, product image right) appears in the guide.

## Quote / caption
No pull quotes broken out; the feature's whole body is an italic quotation, which reads as one giant pull quote at body size. Captions: 15px sans, dark, sentence case, left-aligned to the column; credit on its own line in 12px grey. The opener uses a short heavy dash rule rather than a hairline; the foot uses a full-width hairline above the author block, a small round avatar with the author name, a one-line bio, "Published" date, and "Topics" as underlined text links.

## Guide structure
No numbering. Steps are H4-level subheads in the heavy black serif (about 28px, bold), phrased as questions or imperatives ("What's a Gravel Garden?", "Where Should You Plant a Gravel Garden?", "Complement Your Home Style", "Transform a Small Outdoor Space", "Plant Natives"). Each step is two to four paragraphs of expert quotes; most steps carry one plate after their first or second paragraph, alternating column-width and wide. No lists inside steps. The piece opens with a narrative intro (about six paragraphs) and a product card before the first subhead. Closing device: the last subhead ends the piece; then a newsletter box, then the author block, date and topic links. No summary or "what you need" list.

## Adoptable / not
- [ADAPT: put the plate above rather than beside, on white] The split hero is clean and keeps text off the photo, but the tinted grey panel and the 40/60 split fight the plate-on-white rule; take the type stack (headline / dek / Text by) and stack it under a full-width plate instead.
- [ADOPT] "Text by" label in small grey sans with the name in bold below: a two-line byline that reads as a label pair.
- [ADOPT] Body measure of about 70-75 characters per line at a large serif size; this is the most comfortable reading measure of the four sites.
- [ADOPT] Two plate widths in the body: column-width and wide-contained (about 1.5x the column); no full-bleed needed.
- [ADOPT] Caption left-aligned to the text column edge even when the plate is wider, credit on its own small grey line.
- [ADAPT: swap the heavy dash for a hairline] The short black dash rule as the opener between hero and body is a good "start of body" device; render it as a short hairline in the medium weight.
- [ADOPT] Bold-label spec list (Location, Architect, Footprint, Builder, Photographer) for home-tour metadata; set the labels as tracked caps rather than bold serif.
- [REJECT: no icons] Expand and heart buttons on every plate, the share and Save floating buttons, the copy icon on the hero.
- [REJECT: no boxes/cards/radius] The thumbnail strip box and blue "View 15 Photos" button, the grey newsletter box, the product card with pill button, the grey ad bands.
- [REJECT: no scrims] None on article pages, but the pink promotional ribbon above the nav and the grey hero panel are colour fields behind type; keep type on white.
- [ADAPT: interleave] The feature dumps all fifteen plates after the text; for a home-tour template distribute the plates through the text every two to three paragraphs as AD and TMH do, and keep the credit-per-plate habit.
- [ADAPT: add step numbers] Dwell's unnumbered bold-serif subheads work, but a tracked-caps "01" label above each subhead would make the guide template read as a guide rather than an essay.
- [ADOPT] Author block at the foot: hairline, small portrait, name, one-line bio, published date, topics as underlined text links.
