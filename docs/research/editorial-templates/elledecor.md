## Hero pattern
Two hero variants exist on the same Hearst template.

Interview (split hero): the page is divided in half. The left half is white and holds, centred within it: a tiny underlined category link "design + decorate" in grey sans; the headline in a light humanist sans at about 44px, centred, two lines ("5 Questions with Silvia Venturini Fendi"); a dek in an italic high-contrast display serif (Didot-like) at about 22px; a byline row "BY SEAN SANTIAGO   PUBLISHED: DEC 13, 2022" in tiny tracked caps; and a pill-shaped "SAVE ARTICLE | share" button. The right half is a full-height, edge-bleeding portrait of Fendi standing in the showroom, cropped tight to the viewport (roughly 690px wide by 500px visible), no caption in the hero; the caption ("Above: Silvia Venturini Fendi at Palazzo della Civilta Italiana...") appears as an italic line at the top of the body instead. Portrait lead: yes, three-quarter length, environmental. The subject's name is in the headline, and the count ("5 Questions") acts as the interview signal; there is no "Interview" kicker.

Note (full-bleed hero): a single landscape photograph spans the full viewport width (about 16:9 crop), with the credit "Courtesy Graham & Brown" in tiny bold sans immediately under its bottom-left corner. Below, centred: category link, headline in bold sans ALL CAPS at about 34px, italic serif dek, byline and date, save pill, then a "2 MIN READ" line with a clock icon before the body. The caps headline is a per-article style option in the Hearst CMS, not a note-specific template.

## Body column
The body sits in a column about 570px wide, left of centre, with a 300px advertising rail on the right; the column measures about 65-70 characters per line in a serif text face (a Georgia-like face) at about 19px. Drop cap: yes, a three-line sans drop cap on the first paragraph of both pieces ("W" and "R"). The interview's intro is a single paragraph of editorial voice, preceded by a hairline rule (and the italic hero caption). No rule between the intro and the first question.

## Interview structure
The first exchange uses full names in bold: "ELLE DECOR:" in bold caps followed by the question ALSO in bold, then on a new paragraph "Silvia Venturini Fendi:" in bold followed by the answer in regular weight. From the second exchange on, the prefixes shorten to initials, "ED:" and "SVF:", still bold, with the question text bold and the answer regular. Question and answer are separate paragraphs with a full blank line between them and a slightly larger gap before the next question. Only five exchanges (the "5 Questions" format). Inline images: two column-width photographs sit between exchanges 1-2 and 3-4, each with a tiny grey sans credit line ABOVE the caption and a regular serif caption ("Fendi Casa's new Miami flagship."). No pull quotes. Closing device: an italic line "This conversation has been edited and condensed for clarity and length." followed by an author block (circular headshot, name link, title "Deputy Editor", short bio, "Read full bio").

## Note structure
Header scale is the same as the feature (the caps headline is actually larger and louder than the interview's light headline). Lead image: yes, full bleed. Measure: identical column. About six short paragraphs (one to three sentences each), each separated by a column-width image with credit and caption, so the piece reads as alternating text and plates. Closing device: a one-line quote as the kicker ("Vibrant. Immersive. Timeless.") then the same author bio block. The note uses the feature template unchanged; the only lightness comes from paragraph count.

## Quote / caption
No pull quotes on either page. Captions are two-part: a credit in tiny grey bold sans (about 11px) directly under the image, then a regular serif caption (about 16px) on its own line beneath, left-aligned to the column. The hero caption is relocated to an italic "Above:" line at the head of the body rather than under the image.

## Adoptable / not
- [ADOPT] Full-name prefixes on the first exchange, initials thereafter, so the reader learns who "ED" and "SVF" are without repeating full names ten times.
- [ADOPT] Credit and caption as two separate lines under the plate: credit tiny, caption regular; this fits our three-hairline system if we add a light hairline above the credit.
- [ADOPT] The italic closing line "This conversation has been edited and condensed" as the standard interview sign-off.
- [ADOPT] "5 Questions with ..." as a headline formula for short interviews; it makes the format legible without a kicker.
- [ADAPT: place the caption under the plate instead of an "Above:" line in the body] Relocated hero caption.
- [ADAPT: split hero (headline left, portrait right) is close to our plate-beside-headline pattern, but the photo must not bleed to the edge; give it margins so it reads as a plate] Split hero.
- [ADAPT: bold questions are fine, but drop the bold on the speaker prefix and use small caps instead] Bold caps "ELLE DECOR:" reads as shouting at our scale.
- [ADAPT: italic serif dek to our small caps sans standfirst] Dek style.
- [REJECT: no icons] Clock icon on "2 min read", bookmark and share icons in the save pill, Pinterest badge on the hero, magnifier in the search field.
- [REJECT: no boxes/cards/radius] The pill "SAVE ARTICLE" button, the rounded "RELATED" chips in the sticky bar, the boxed newsletter panel.
- [REJECT: text never over photos / no scrims] The dark full-width cookie notice and the blue newsletter overlay that slide over the hero.
- [REJECT: no drop caps in our body (AD reference uses none)] Three-line sans drop cap.
